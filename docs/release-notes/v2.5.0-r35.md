# v2.5.0-r35 — loops land on the beat you meant, and they are orange

<!-- debs: rebuild -->

New Mixxx build **and** a skin change — install both.

- **The nearest beat, whether or not it has gone by.** This is the one that
  made loops feel random. Mixxx snapped a loop point to the closest beat only
  while the deck had no loop running. Once one was running — and the *press*
  of an IN or OUT button is what puts the deck in that state, so this covers
  every tap after the first — loop-in jumped to the beat *behind* and loop-out
  to the beat *ahead*. Tap OUT twenty milliseconds after the four, and you got
  a five-beat loop and a deck that was suddenly out of time with the other
  one.

  Both ends now take the beat you were nearest when you hit the button. The
  only time either end looks the other way is when the nearest beat would put
  it on top of the other end of the loop, or past the end of the track.

- **And the track keeps its place in the bar.** Snapping *back* to a beat you
  have just played sounds like it should make the deck stutter. It does not:
  when a loop point moves behind the play position, Mixxx carries the play
  position into the new loop in whole loop lengths, so it lands the same
  distance into the bar as it was. You hear the loop take hold, not a jump.

- **SHIFT + LOOP IN could kill the platter.** The mapping's check for "is
  there a loop to adjust?" was written so that it never actually looked, so
  SHIFT + LOOP IN (or OUT) on a deck with no loop armed the jog-wheel loop
  adjust anyway — and nothing clears that until a loop has been switched on
  and off again. While it is armed the platter top stops scratching
  altogether, and the moment you do make a loop the wheel drags the loop's in
  point around instead of nudging the track. That is most of "sometimes loops
  just bug". It now checks, and it also disarms itself whenever a deck loads
  a track.

- **A loop-adjust can no longer wipe the loop out.** Turning the jog wheel in
  loop-adjust mode wrote the new position straight through, and Mixxx throws
  the whole loop away the instant the in point reaches the out point — one
  flick of the platter on a short loop was enough to make the loop vanish and
  the track play straight on. The adjust now stops a hair short of the other
  end, and the out point can no longer be pushed past the end of the track.

- **QUANTIZE comes up on, on both decks.** A loop that is not on the grid is
  the other way a loop ends up out of time, and Mixxx ships quantise *off*
  per deck and then remembers whatever it was last set to — so a rig can
  easily be sitting with one deck quantised and the other not, which is the
  rest of "sometimes". The DDJ-400 mapping now switches it on for both decks
  at every start, the way a Pioneer player boots. The QUANTIZE button beside
  the waveform still turns it off for the rest of the session if you want a
  loop off the grid.

- **Loops are orange.** Everything to do with a loop now uses Pioneer's
  orange instead of the green the skin borrowed from the play button: the
  loop region and the LOOP IN / LOOP OUT markers on the scrolling waveform,
  the loop region on both deck overviews, and the loop-size readout beside
  the waveform. A loop that is set but not running shows as a dim orange
  band rather than a grey one.

## Install (on the Pi)

```
sudo update-pioneered.sh
```

or Cog → **SOFTWARE UPDATE** → UPDATE, twice.

## Field checks

1. **QUANTIZE is lit on both decks** after a fresh start.
2. Play a track. Tap **LOOP IN** a fraction *after* a beat, then **LOOP OUT**
   a fraction after the beat four later: you get a four-beat loop, both ends
   on the grid, and the deck stays in time with the other deck. Repeat
   tapping a fraction *before* each beat — same loop.
3. With that loop running, tap **LOOP OUT** again shortly after a beat: the
   loop ends on that beat, not on the next one, and the music does not
   stutter as it shortens.
4. **RELOOP/EXIT** leaves the loop and re-enters it on the beat.
5. The **beat-loop pads** (1/4 through 32) still take hold immediately and in
   time.
6. **SHIFT + LOOP IN with no loop on the deck**: nothing happens, and the
   platter still scratches afterwards. (Before this build it would go deaf.)
7. Make a loop, **SHIFT + LOOP IN**, and wind the jog wheel forward hard: the
   in point walks up to just short of the out point and stops. The loop
   survives. Same with **SHIFT + LOOP OUT** wound backwards, and wound
   forwards past the end of the track.
8. Loading a new track while a loop adjust is armed leaves the platter
   working.
9. The loop region and its markers are **orange** on the waveform and on both
   overviews, and the loop-size number beside the waveform is orange too.
   Exiting a loop leaves a dim orange band, not a grey one.
10. Hot cues, the beatgrid's red bar markers and the BPM readout are all
    unchanged.

## Note

This build has not been run on hardware. The patch applies and round-trips
cleanly against the tree the rest of the series builds, and the mapping
script parses, but everything above is a check to perform, not a check that
has been performed.

One of Mixxx's own loop tests moves with this change —
`LoopInOutButtons_QuantizeEnabled` shrinks a loop by holding OUT over a beat
jump, and it relied on the old "snap to the next beat" rule to move the out
point at all. It now lets the jump happen before releasing the button; the
assertions are unchanged. See `mixxx-patch/VERSION.md` entry 27. Nothing was
skipped or disabled.

Check 3 is the one worth doing properly — it is the whole reason the
quantise rule changed.
